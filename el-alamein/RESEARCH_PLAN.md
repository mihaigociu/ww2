# Research Plan: Battle of El-Alamein — Educational Document for High School Students

**Project goal:** Produce a comprehensive, accurate, and engaging Markdown document about the Battle of El-Alamein, targeted at high-school students. The document must include maps/images, cited sources, and cover both the First and Second Battles.

**Date:** 2026-04-22  
**Author:** Claude Code (claude-sonnet-4-6)

## 1. Research Questions

The document must answer the following questions clearly for a high-school audience:

| # | Question | Why it matters |
|---|----------|---------------|
| 1 | What was the strategic situation in North Africa before El-Alamein? | Context for why the battle mattered |
| 2 | What were the First and Second Battles of El-Alamein (dates, phases, commanders)? | Core factual narrative |
| 3 | Who were the key commanders — Rommel and Montgomery — and how did their decisions shape outcomes? | Human story; character study |
| 4 | What role did logistics and intelligence (Ultra) play? | Analytical depth |
| 5 | Which Allied nations contributed, and what was their role? | Diversity / Commonwealth angle |
| 6 | What was the human cost (casualties)? | Impact and empathy |
| 7 | Why is El-Alamein considered a turning point in WWII? | Historical significance |
| 8 | What happened after El-Alamein (immediate aftermath)? | Closure / forward context |

## 8. Notes on Audience

High-school students (ages 14–18) respond best to:
- **Narrative storytelling** — battles as human stories, not lists of dates
- **Concrete comparisons** — e.g., "the Eighth Army had X more tanks than Rommel"
- **Quotes** — Churchill, soldiers' diaries, commanders
- **Maps** — visual anchors to understand spatial movement
- **Short paragraphs** — max 4–5 sentences per paragraph
- **Clear signposting** — headers, bold key terms, a glossary

The document should NOT read like a Wikipedia article. It should feel like a well-written textbook chapter or a National Geographic feature.
